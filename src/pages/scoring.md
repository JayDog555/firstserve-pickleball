Scoring is the most common thing new players ask about after their first game. This guide explains it in plain language. It works in any US city and sticks to what the official rules say.

The scoring rules below come from the USA Pickleball [rules summary](https://usapickleball.org/rules/summary/). For full detail, see the [official rulebook](https://usapickleball.org/rules/). Tips marked **common practice** are not rules.

## The short version

- Games are normally played to 11 points, and you must win by 2.
- In traditional scoring, only the serving team can score a point.
- In doubles, both players on a team get a turn to serve before the serve goes to the other team. The first service turn of each game is the exception.
- The score is called as three numbers in doubles: your score, their score, then the server number.

## Side-out scoring

Most recreational play uses traditional side-out scoring.

- **Points are scored only by the serving team.** If the receiving team wins the rally, they score nothing. They just get closer to winning the serve back.
- **A fault by the receiving team** gives the serving team a point.
- **A fault by the serving team** means that server loses the serve.
- A "side out" is when the serve passes to the other team.

Tournament games may be played to 15 or 21, still win by 2. Open play games are usually to 11.

## Server one and server two (doubles)

- When your team gets the serve, the player on the right-hand side serves first. That player is "server one" for this turn.
- Each time your team wins a point, the server switches sides with their partner and serves again, now from the other side.
- When your team loses a rally, the partner becomes "server two" and serves from their current side.
- When server two loses a rally, it is a side out. The other team now serves, starting from the right-hand side.

## Why games start at "zero, zero, two"

At the start of each game, only one player on the serving team gets to serve before the serve passes to the other team. That keeps the first team from getting a full two-server turn.

To show this, the first server of the game is called "server two." So the first score call of every doubles game is "zero, zero, two." Our [etiquette guide](/guides/etiquette/) cites the rulebook section for this.

## Calling the score

In doubles, the server calls three numbers before each serve:

1. The serving team's score
2. The receiving team's score
3. The server number, 1 or 2

Example: "four, two, one" means the serving team has 4, the receiving team has 2, and the first server of this turn is serving.

In singles, the server calls two numbers: their own score, then the receiver's score.

**Common practice:** call the score loudly enough for all four players to hear. If you lose track, ask before the serve. Most groups would rather pause than argue later.

## Where to stand: even and odd scores

This is the trick that helps you check the score.

- **Doubles:** when your team's score is even (0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10), the player who served first for your team in that game stands on the right side when serving or receiving. When the score is odd (1, 3, 5, 7, 9), that player stands on the left.
- **Singles:** serve from the right when your score is even and from the left when it is odd.

If the players are not where the score says they should be, the score call or the positions are wrong. Sort it out before the next serve.

## Rally scoring

Some leagues and events use rally scoring instead.

- A point is scored after every rally, no matter which team served.
- Games tend to move faster, because every rally changes the score.

**Common practice:** open play groups and leagues set their own format. If a game is using rally scoring, someone will usually say so before the first serve. If no one says, ask.

## Common questions

**Can the receiving team score?** Not in traditional side-out scoring. Only the serving team scores. In rally scoring, either team can score on any rally.

**Why did my partner serve first last time but I serve first now?** Server one is whoever is on the right-hand side when your team gets the serve back. That changes as players switch sides after points.

**What does "side out" mean?** The serve passes to the other team.

**Do I switch sides when I win a point as the receiving team?** No. In side-out scoring, only the server switches sides, and only after winning a point.

## Quick summary

1. Play to 11, win by 2.
2. In side-out scoring, only the serving team scores.
3. Each doubles team gets two servers per turn, except the first turn of the game.
4. Call three numbers: serving score, receiving score, server number.
5. Even score: first server on the right. Odd score: first server on the left.
