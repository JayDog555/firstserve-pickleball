This guide explains the pickleball kitchen rule in plain language. This guide works in any US city and sticks to what the official rules say.

Every rule below comes from the USA Pickleball [rules summary](https://usapickleball.org/rules/summary/). For full detail, see the [official rulebook](https://usapickleball.org/rules/). Tips marked **common practice** are not rules.

## What the kitchen is

- "The kitchen" is the common name for the **non-volley zone**.
- It is the area within 7 feet of the net, on both sides.
- The lines around it count as part of the zone.

## The one thing you cannot do

You cannot **volley** in the kitchen. A volley is hitting the ball before it bounces.

It is a fault if you volley while:

- standing in the kitchen, or
- touching the kitchen line.

## What you can do

- You can stand in the kitchen any time you are not volleying. The rules summary says so directly.
- If the ball bounces in the kitchen, you can step in and hit it. That is not a volley, because the ball bounced first.
- After a bounce shot from inside the kitchen, step back out before you volley again.

## The momentum rule

- If you volley near the line and your momentum carries you into the kitchen, it is a fault.
- It counts if anything you are wearing or carrying touches the zone. That includes your paddle, a hat, or sunglasses.
- It is still a fault even if the ball is already dead when you touch the zone. Finishing the point first does not save you.

**Common practice:** if you volley close to the line, hold your balance for a moment before you move forward.

## Serves and the kitchen line

- A serve must land in the court diagonally across from you.
- A serve that touches the kitchen line is short. It is a fault.
- On every other shot, a ball that touches any line is in.

## How the two-bounce rule connects

- The receiving team must let the serve bounce before hitting it.
- Then the serving team must let the return bounce before hitting it.
- After those two bounces, either team can volley, as long as they are outside the kitchen.

**Common practice:** once both bounces are done, many players move up to the kitchen line and trade soft shots.

## Common questions

**Can I walk through the kitchen to get a short ball?** Yes. You can be in the kitchen any time you are not volleying.

**Can my toe be on the line when I volley?** No. The line is part of the zone, so a volley with a foot on the line is a fault.

**Does my paddle count if it touches the kitchen after a volley?** Yes. Anything you are wearing or carrying counts.

**Who calls kitchen faults in open play?** **Common practice:** players usually call their own faults, and opponents may point out what they saw. See our [etiquette guide](/guides/etiquette/) for line calls and calling the score.

## Quick summary

1. The kitchen is the 7-foot zone on each side of the net, lines included.
2. No volleys while touching the zone.
3. Standing in it is fine when you are not volleying.
4. Momentum into the zone after a volley is a fault, even after the ball is dead.
5. A serve that touches the kitchen line is a fault.
