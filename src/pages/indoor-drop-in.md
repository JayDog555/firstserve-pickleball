Indoor drop-in is open play in a gym or indoor facility during set hours. It is a good option when it is hot, cold, wet, or dark outside. This guide works in any US city. Each venue sets its own rules, so the venue's own page and the front desk come first.

## Kinds of indoor venues

**City and county recreation centers.** Many parks departments run drop-in pickleball in community center gyms. Courts are often taped or painted on a basketball floor, and nets may be portable. Sessions usually run in fixed weekday or evening windows that change by season.

**YMCAs and similar nonprofits.** Many YMCAs, JCCs, and community nonprofits offer pickleball in their gyms. Some sessions are for members only. Others allow guests with a day pass. Check the program schedule or ask the front desk.

**Dedicated indoor pickleball clubs.** These facilities are built for pickleball, with permanent courts and nets. They often sell memberships, drop-in passes, and court reservations, and many run sessions sorted by skill level.

**Gyms and athletic clubs.** Some fitness clubs and multi-sport facilities add pickleball to a basketball court or a converted space. Access is usually tied to a membership or a guest pass.

## How sign-ups, day passes, and memberships usually work

Formats vary, but most indoor drop-in uses one or more of these:

- **Walk-in during posted hours.** You show up, check in at the desk, and join the paddle queue.
- **Advance sign-up.** Some venues cap the number of players per session and ask you to register online or by phone. Popular sessions can fill.
- **Day pass or drop-in fee.** You pay for a single visit. Public rec centers often charge less than private clubs, and some charge nothing.
- **Membership or annual pass.** Regular players may pay for a monthly or yearly pass. Some public programs offer lower rates to residents.
- **Waiver and ID.** Many venues ask first-time visitors to sign a liability waiver and show ID or a membership card.

Fees and rules change, so check the current price on the venue's own page before you go.

## What to ask before your first visit

- **Which sessions are open to beginners?** Some time slots are labeled beginner, social, or by rating.
- **Do I need to sign up ahead, or can I walk in?**
- **What does it cost for a non-member or first-time visitor?**
- **Do I need to sign a waiver or bring ID?**
- **Are paddles and balls provided, or can I borrow a paddle?**
- **What shoes are allowed on the floor?**
- **Is the session canceled during school breaks, holidays, or events?** Gyms in rec centers and schools are often shared.

## Indoor balls

Indoor balls are softer and have larger holes. Outdoor balls are harder and have smaller holes. An outdoor ball can bounce and skid differently on a gym floor. Many indoor sessions supply balls or pick one ball for the group. If you bring your own, bring indoor balls and ask which one the group uses.

## Non-marking court shoes

Most indoor facilities ask for non-marking soles to protect the floor. Court shoes made for pickleball, tennis, volleyball, or basketball usually work. Running shoes are built for forward motion and give less side support. Some venues ask you to wear clean shoes that you did not wear outside. If you are not sure, ask the front desk. ## Paddle stacks and rotation indoors

Indoor drop-in often has more players than courts, so a rotation system keeps it fair. Common versions:

- **Paddle rack or stack.** You place your paddle in a rack or line by the court. The next four paddles take the next open court.
- **Whiteboard or sign-up sheet.** You write your name and wait to be called.
- **Timed rotation or app check-in.** Some clubs use a timer, a lighted board, or a check-in app to assign courts.

Courtesies that apply almost everywhere:

1. Ask how the rotation works before you set your paddle down.
2. Do not save spots for friends who are not there yet.
3. Play one game, often to 11, then leave the court and rejoin the line.
4. Stay off the court until a game ends, and walk behind courts, not across them.
5. If a ball rolls onto another court, say "ball" and wait for play to stop.
6. Tell your group you are new. Most players will tell you the score format and where to stand.

The posted rules always win over habit. Our guide to [finding beginner-friendly open play](/guides/find-open-play/) covers paddle stacking and skill levels in more detail.

## How to find indoor drop-in near you

1. Search your city or county parks site for "pickleball" and look for a community center or gym schedule. Our [open play guide](/guides/find-open-play/) explains how to find and read these pages.
2. Check the YMCA or community center closest to you for a gym schedule.
3. Look up indoor pickleball clubs in your area and read their own sites for drop-in passes and beginner sessions.
4. Browse our [US city list](/cities/). Each live city page links to the official source for every venue.
