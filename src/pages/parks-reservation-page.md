City and county parks pages tell you when and how you can use a public pickleball court. They are often written for staff and permit holders, so the useful parts can be hard to spot. This guide works in any US city. It covers the fields that matter most for a beginner, and what to do when a page leaves something out.

The official page always wins over this guide, a court app, or a friend's advice. Rules change by season and by park.

## Start with the court type

Most pages describe courts in one of three ways:

- **Dedicated pickleball courts.** Permanent lines and nets set up for pickleball. These are the easiest place for a beginner to start.
- **Shared or overlay courts.** Tennis courts with pickleball lines added. You may need portable nets, and the page may say tennis players have priority. Some pages call these "striped for both tennis and pickleball."
- **Indoor gym courts.** Lines taped or painted on a gym floor at a recreation center. Access is usually tied to a schedule, a pass, or a membership. See our [indoor drop-in guide](/guides/indoor-drop-in/) for how these work.

Note the number of courts. A park with two courts fills faster than a complex with twenty.

## First-come vs. reservable courts

This is the most important line on the page. Look for words such as:

- **First-come, first-served, walk-on, or drop-in.** You show up and play. There is no booking. If people are waiting, a paddle rack or rotation usually decides who plays next.
- **Reservable or bookable.** Someone can pay to hold the court for a set time. Pages often list which court numbers can be reserved and which stay open.
- **Not reservable.** No bookings at all. Everyone uses the queue.

Many parks mix the two. For example, Clark County, NV says its [pickleball courts](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/parks___recreation/facilities/pickle-ball-courts) are first-come, first-serve and cannot be reserved, except at Sunset Park. At Sunset Park, the county's [drop-in reservation page](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/parks___recreation/sports/pickleball/pickleball-drop-in-reservation-information) lists courts 13 to 16 and 21 to 24 as reservable, and says courts that are not reserved are available first-come, first-serve.

If you plan to walk on, know which court numbers can be booked. Otherwise a reservation holder may arrive in the middle of your game.

## Open play vs. private booking

These are different things, and pages do not always label them clearly.

- **Open play** is a group session where anyone can join and rotate in. It may be free, or it may require a program registration or a pass. Some cities list open play in their recreation catalog instead of on the park page.
- **A private booking** holds a court for you and your group. Other players do not rotate in.
- **Leagues, classes, and clinics** are separate programs with their own sign-up and fees. They often take priority over walk-on play during their scheduled times.

If you are new, open play or a beginner class is usually the best first step. Our [open play guide](/guides/find-open-play/) explains paddle stacking and skill levels.

## Resident and non-resident fees

Fees are often listed per court per hour, per person, or per session. Look for:

- **Resident and non-resident rates.** Some cities charge non-residents more, or let residents book earlier.
- **What the fee covers.** A court fee may not include lights, parking, or equipment.
- **Free walk-on time.** A park can charge for reservations and still leave walk-on play free.
- **Proof of residency.** Resident rates may require an account, an ID, or a utility bill.

If a fee is not listed, do not assume it is free. Call the number on the page.

## Booking windows and limits

The booking window is how far ahead you can reserve. Pages may say "7 days in advance," "opens at 8 a.m.," or list a date each month when the next month opens. For example, Clark County says Sunset Park bookings for the next month open on the last Wednesday of the current month.

Pages also list limits, such as a maximum number of courts, hours per booking, or bookings per month. At Sunset Park, Clark County limits reservations to 2 courts and 2 hours, and caps the number of reservations per month. Some systems require an account set up before booking day, so create it early.

## Cancellation and refund rules

Read this section before you pay. Common rules include:

- A cutoff, such as 24 or 48 hours before your time, to cancel for a refund or credit.
- A processing fee for cancellations.
- No refunds at all for some bookings. For example, Clark County says no cancellations, changes, credits, or refunds are granted for online Sunset Park reservations.
- Weather rules. Some cities give a credit for rain or heat closures. Others do not.

If the page does not say, ask before you book.

## Hours, lights, and closures

Park hours, court hours, and reservation hours are not always the same. Lights may run on a timer that ends before the park closes. Look for a closures page or alert banner, and for posted maintenance days. Our [what to bring](/guides/what-to-bring/) checklist covers heat, sun, and layers.

## How to book by phone or in person

Not every city has an online system. Some take reservations only by phone, by email form, or at a sports office. For example, the City of Henderson, NV [pickleball page](https://www.cityofhenderson.com/government/departments/parks-and-recreation/parks-and-trails/pickleball) says courts can be reserved by calling its Sports Office. When you call, have your preferred date, time, park, and number of courts ready.

## A quick checklist

Before you go, you should be able to answer these from the official page:

1. Are the courts dedicated, shared with tennis, or indoor?
2. Are they first-come, reservable, or a mix? Which court numbers?
3. Is there open play, and do you need to register for it?
4. What does it cost, and is there a resident rate?
5. When does booking open, and what are the limits?
6. What happens if you cancel, or if weather closes the courts?
7. What are the court hours, and are there lights?

If you cannot answer one of these, call the phone number on the page. Pages can be out of date.
