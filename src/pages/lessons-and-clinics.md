A lesson or a beginner clinic is not required before your first open play, but many new players find that one short session makes open play easier. This guide works in any US city. It explains the common formats, where to look, and what to ask before you pay for anything.

## Three common formats

**Beginner intro sessions.** Often called "Intro to Pickleball," "Learn to Play," or "Pickleball 101." These are usually short, low-cost or free sessions run by a parks department, community center, club, or facility. They cover the rules, scoring, and basic strokes, and some lend paddles and balls. They are a good fit if you have never played.

**Group clinics.** A coach works with a small group, usually grouped by skill level, on one topic or a short series of topics: serves and returns, dinking, the third shot, or positioning. Clinics can be a single session or a multi-week class. They cost less per person than a private lesson, and you get to practice with other players at your level.

**Private or semi-private lessons.** One coach with one player, or with two to four players who book together. You get direct feedback on your own strokes and habits. Private lessons usually cost the most per hour, so many players take one or two after a clinic, once they know what they want to work on.

There is no single right order. A common path is an intro session, then a group clinic or two, then open play, with a private lesson later if you want help with a specific habit.

## Where to find them

### Your city or county parks and recreation catalog

Many parks and recreation departments publish a seasonal activity guide or online class catalog. Search the catalog for "pickleball" and look for words like beginner, intro, or level 1. Classes listed there are usually registered in advance through the department's own registration site. Our [guide to finding open play](/guides/find-open-play/) explains how to find your city or county parks page.

### YMCAs and community centers

Many YMCAs, community centers, and senior centers run beginner pickleball programs in their gyms. Some programs are for members only and some are open to the public, so check the program listing or call the front desk.

### Clubs and pickleball facilities

Private clubs, tennis clubs, and dedicated pickleball facilities often run clinics, beginner programs, and private lessons with staff coaches. Some require a membership and some sell drop-in or guest access. The facility's own website or front desk is the source for current schedules and prices.

### USA Pickleball resources

[USA Pickleball](https://usapickleball.org/) is the national governing body for the sport in the United States. Its site has rules, skill level information, and a [coaching page](https://usapickleball.org/coaching/) that lists its official coach education and certification partners: the Professional Pickleball Registry (PPR), Pickleball Coaching International (PCI), and the Racquet Sports Professionals Association (RSPA).

### Certified coaches

Certification does not guarantee a good fit, but it tells you a coach has completed a structured program. Organizations that certify pickleball instructors include:

- [Professional Pickleball Registry (PPR)](https://pprpickleball.org/), an official education and certification partner of USA Pickleball.
- [International Pickleball Teaching Professional Association (IPTPA)](https://www.iptpa.com/), an instructor certification organization.

If a coach lists a certification, it is reasonable to ask which one and at what level.

## What to ask before you book

- **Who is it for?** Is the session labeled for true beginners, or for players who already know the rules and can rally?
- **How many players per coach?** Smaller groups usually mean more time hitting balls.
- **How long is it, and is it one session or a series?**
- **What does it cost, and what is included?** Ask about court fees, membership or guest fees, and whether balls are provided.
- **Can I borrow a paddle?** If you do not own one yet, ask whether loaners are available.
- **Indoor or outdoor?** This affects shoes, the type of ball, and what you wear.
- **What is the cancellation or weather policy?** Outdoor sessions may be canceled for rain or heat.
- **Who is the coach?** Ask about their teaching experience and any certification.

## What a first clinic usually covers

Formats vary by coach and venue, but a first beginner session often includes:

- How to hold the paddle, usually a relaxed continental grip
- Court layout, including the non-volley zone (the kitchen)
- The serve and the two-bounce rule
- Basic scoring and calling the score
- Forehands, backhands, and a first look at dinking near the net
- Short drills, then a few practice games
- How open play works, including paddle stacking and rotating partners

You do not need to master any of this in one session. The goal is to leave knowing enough to join a beginner game without feeling lost.

## What to bring

Wear court or tennis shoes and comfortable athletic clothes, and bring water. Bring a paddle if you have one, or ask whether loaners are provided.
