Most open play groups welcome new players. A few habits make that easier for everyone. This guide works in any US city. It separates two kinds of advice:

- **Rules** come from the official [USA Pickleball rulebook](https://usapickleball.org/rules/). Each rule below links to the 2026 rulebook or the USA Pickleball rules summary.
- **Common practice** is what many open play groups do. It is not a rule, and it varies by court. The posted sign at your venue always comes first.

The rulebook sets the tone in its underlying principles: pickleball "requires cooperation and courtesy," and giving the opponent the benefit of any doubt is part of fair play ([2026 Official Rulebook](https://usapickleball.org/docs/rules/USAP-Official-Rulebook.pdf), Section 1).

## Paddle stacking and rotation (common practice)

Busy courts use a paddle queue so everyone gets a turn. The details vary by venue.

- Place your paddle in the rack or line when you arrive. That is your place in the queue.
- When a court opens, the next four paddles usually play doubles.
- When your game ends, leave the court and put your paddle at the back of the line.
- Some groups use "2 on, 2 off" (winners stay) when the queue is short, and "4 on, 4 off" when it is long. Ask before you assume.
- Do not move someone else's paddle, and do not save a spot for a friend who has not arrived.

Our [open play guide](/guides/find-open-play/) covers the queue step by step.

## Calling the score (rule)

The [2026 Official Rulebook](https://usapickleball.org/docs/rules/USAP-Official-Rulebook.pdf) covers this in Section 6:

- **The server normally calls the score** (Rule 6.B), and only once all players are, or should be, ready.
- **Doubles uses three numbers:** serving team's score, receiving team's score, then the server number. A game starts at "zero, zero, two" (Rule 6.B.2).
- **Singles uses two numbers:** server's score, then receiver's score (Rule 6.B.1).
- **Call the whole score before you serve.** Serving while the score is still being called is a fault (Rules 6.C and 6.C.1).

The USA Pickleball [rules summary](https://usapickleball.org/rules/summary/) says games are normally played to 11 points, win by 2. Call the score loudly enough for the other side to hear. If you lose track, ask. Rule 6.E.1 lets a player ask the opponent for the score before the serve.

## Line calls (rule)

Section 8 of the [2026 Official Rulebook](https://usapickleball.org/docs/rules/USAP-Official-Rulebook.pdf) sets the line-calling rules for recreational play:

- **You call the lines on your own end of the court** (Rule 8.A). In doubles, either partner may make the call.
- **A ball on the line is in**, except a serve that lands on the non-volley zone line, which is out ([rules summary](https://usapickleball.org/rules/summary/); Rules 8.B and 8.C).
- **Give the benefit of the doubt.** Questionable calls go in favor of the opponent, and any ball you cannot promptly call "out" is "in" (Rule 8.D).
- **Only call "out" if you see space** between the line and the ball where it lands (Rule 8.E).
- **Make the call promptly**, by voice, a hand signal, or both (Rule 8.F).
- **If partners disagree, the ball is in** (Rule 8.H).
- **You may ask your opponent** for their view of a call on your side. If they make a clear call, it stands (Rule 8.I).
- **Spectators are not consulted** on any call (Rule 8.J). If you are waiting by the fence, stay out of it.

## Waiting your turn (common practice, sometimes posted)

- **Wait outside the gate or fence** until the game on court ends. Some parks post this. For example, Portland Parks & Recreation's [pickleball page](https://www.portland.gov/parks/recreation/pickleball) asks players to wait outside the fence for others to finish.
- **Enter between points, not during a rally.** Wait for a pause, then walk on quickly.
- **Do not walk behind an active court** during a rally. Wait until the point ends, then cross along the fence line.
- **Do not hold a court for a missing partner.** Portland's page says single players may not hold a court until their partner arrives.

## Stray balls and other courts (rule and common practice)

The rulebook defines a hinder as a transient element not caused by a player that affects play, and lists stray balls and players from another court as examples (2026 Official Rulebook, Section 2). Any player may call a hinder, and the rally is replayed (Rules 9.C.1 and 9.C.2).

In practice:

- If your ball rolls onto another court, **call "ball on court"** and wait for their rally to end. Do not run onto an active court to get it.
- If a ball rolls onto your court, **stop play**, and roll or toss it back gently once the point ends.
- If the next court is between points, a short "Thank you" when they return your ball is enough.

## Sportsmanship (common practice)

- Tap paddles at the net or say "good game" when a game ends.
- Do not coach other players unless they ask.
- Own your faults. The rulebook lets players override their own call or a partner's call to their own disadvantage (Rule 8.G).
- Keep disagreements short. If a call is unclear, replay the point and move on.

## Playing with mixed levels (common practice)

Open play often mixes beginners and stronger players.

- **Say you are new** before the first serve. Most players adjust.
- **Pick the right court.** If courts are labeled by level, play at your level. Our [ratings and skill levels guide](/guides/ratings-and-skill-levels/) explains what 2.0 to 3.5 mean.
- **If you are the stronger player,** keep the ball in play and avoid aiming hard shots at a new player.
- **If you are the newer player,** do not apologize for every miss. Play the next point.

## Phones and noise (common practice)

- Keep your phone on silent and off the court surface.
- Take calls away from the courts, not at the fence beside a game.
- Cheering is fine. Long conversations at the baseline of an active court are not.
- Some parks post hours or noise rules. Follow the sign and the official parks page.

## Court time limits when people are waiting

Many public courts post a time limit. The posted sign or official page sets the limit, not habit. Two examples:

- Portland Parks & Recreation's [pickleball page](https://www.portland.gov/parks/recreation/pickleball) asks players to limit play to one hour if others are waiting.
- Seattle Parks and Recreation's [pickleball page](https://www.seattle.gov/parks/sports/pickleball) says unbooked courts are open for drop-in use, first come, first served, for a 90-minute maximum, and drop-in players must yield to someone with proof of a reservation.

If no limit is posted, a common practice is one game per group when people are waiting, then rotate off. Our guide to [reading a parks reservation page](/guides/parks-reservation-page/) explains reserved vs. drop-in courts.

## A short checklist for your first session

1. Read the sign at the gate and the venue's official page.
2. Put your paddle in the queue and wait outside the court.
3. Say you are new.
4. Call the score before you serve, and call your own lines fairly.
5. Leave the court when the game ends.
